\section*{Introduction}
Expected goals measures how good a chance was, not whether shooting beat keeping the ball.
Comparing the two is hard because players shoot when chances already look good. Recent
tracking-data models recommend when to shoot, without causal claims (Verronneau et al., MLSA
2026). We ask: when players shot, did it pay off compared with what they would typically
have done instead?


\section*{Methods}
We use StatsBomb open event data with 360 freeze-frames (player positions). Decisions are
live-play actions in the attacking third with the ball's 3~m surroundings fully visible.
Each shot is compared with keeping the ball as players in the same situation actually did:
the observed mix of passes, carries and dribbles, then natural play, later shots included.
We compare only where both choices were realistic (94.9\% of eligible shots). The outcome is
the first goal within 15 seconds ($+$1 scored, $-$1 conceded, 0 otherwise). A doubly robust
estimator, cross-fitted by match, adjusts for geometry, pressure, score, time and recent
actions. The analysis was fixed on Euro 2020 and 2024 (102 matches), then run once on World
Cup 2022 (64 matches).


\section*{Results}
Adjustment matters: shots and other decisions start 2.2 standard deviations apart in
distance to goal; after weighting, every feature in the adjustment set is within 0.08. In
the World Cup (1,228 shots; 20,532 other decisions), the net scoring rate within 15 seconds
was 10.7\% after shots, against an estimated 7.7\% had the same players kept the ball: a
gain of 3.1 percentage points (95\% CI 0.7 to 5.5, clustered by match), well under half the
raw gap of 8.4 (Figure~\ref{fig:effect}a).

The result does not rest on one estimator or one match: other estimators give 3.0 to 4.7,
and dropping any one match or team gives 2.3 to 4.1. With shots randomly reassigned, the
pipeline finds no effect; on 400 simulated datasets, its intervals covered the true effect
93\% of the time.

Four results limit generalisation (Figure~\ref{fig:effect}b). The Euro estimate points the
same way but is smaller and uncertain: 1.8 ($-$0.2 to 3.8). Clustering by opponent, unlike
by team, widens the interval to include zero ($-$0.3 to 6.4), as does a stricter 5~m
visibility rule (2.3; $-$0.5 to 5.0). Hidden factors shifting the odds of shooting 1.35-fold
would bring the estimated lower bound to zero.


\section*{Conclusion}
Under the stated assumptions, the shots players chose in a held-out tournament produced a
modest short-term gain over keeping the ball, stable across estimators, matches and teams.
Its main uncertainties are quantified, not assumed away. The contribution is a reproducible
audit of shot selection, rerunnable on any StatsBomb 360 competition: what shooting added,
and how far that can be trusted.
